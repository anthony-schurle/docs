# Lean-Jacked Tall Physique Routine

## Goal

Build a physique that is not bodybuilder-sized, but clearly muscular, athletic, and filled-out as a tall person.

The target look:

- Broad shoulders
- Full upper chest
- Thick back and lats
- Visible arms
- Strong legs
- Lean waist
- Athletic conditioning

The goal is **not just to be lean**. The goal is to have enough muscle on a tall frame that being lean looks imposing rather than skinny.

---

# Weekly Structure

Recommended split:

| Day       | Focus                            |
| --------- | -------------------------------- |
| Sunday    | Upper Push + Lats                |
| Monday    | Lower Strength                   |
| Tuesday   | Upper Pull + Delts               |
| Wednesday | Zone 2 cardio or rest            |
| Thursday  | Lower Hypertrophy + Athleticism  |
| Friday    | Upper Chest, Shoulders, and Arms |
| Saturday  | Rest                             |

---

# Day 1 — Upper Push + Lats

## 1. Bench Press

**3–4 sets x 4–6 reps**

Purpose:

- Main chest strength builder
- Trains chest, front delts, and triceps
- Gives objective strength progression

---

## 2. Weighted Pull-Up or Heavy Lat Pulldown

**3–4 sets x 5–8 reps**

Purpose:

- Builds lats
- Creates width
- Balances pressing volume

Notes:

- Use pull-ups if you can get clean reps
- Drive elbows down, not just hands toward chest

---

## 3. Incline Dumbbell Press

**3 sets x 8–10 reps**

Purpose:

- Upper chest growth
- Shoulder-friendly pressing
- Better chest stretch than barbell for many lifters

Notes:

- Use a 20–35 degree incline
- Do not turn it into a shoulder press
- Control the bottom position

---

## 4. Lateral Raise

**3 sets x 12–20 reps**

Purpose:

- Side delt growth
- Shoulder width
- Major visual return

Notes:

- Do not ego lift
- Slight bend in elbows
- Raise with control
- Stop around shoulder height or slightly above if comfortable

---

## 5. Triceps Pushdown

**2–3 sets x 10–15 reps**

Purpose:

- Triceps size
- Lockout strength
- Arm fullness

Notes:

- Keep elbows stable
- Fully lock out
- Control the negative

---

## 6. Optional Cable Curl or Incline Dumbbell Curl

**2 sets x 10–15 reps**

Purpose:

- Extra biceps frequency
- Arm fullness
- Better look in long arms

Notes:

- Only include this if time and recovery are good
- Full stretch
- No swinging
- Control the lowering phase

---

# Day 2 — Lower Strength

## 1. Back Squat or Front Squat

**3–4 sets x 4–6 reps**

Purpose:

- Leg strength
- Quads, glutes, core
- Overall athletic base

Notes:

- Use the variation that fits your build best
- Tall lifters may prefer front squats, safety-bar squats, or heeled shoes
- Keep reps clean

---

## 2. Romanian Deadlift

**3 sets x 6–10 reps**

Purpose:

- Hamstrings
- Glutes
- Posterior chain
- Athletic hinge strength

Notes:

- Hips back
- Soft knees
- Keep back neutral
- Feel the stretch in the hamstrings

---

## 3. Leg Press

**2–3 sets x 8–12 reps**

Purpose:

- Leg size without excessive spinal loading
- Quad and glute hypertrophy

Notes:

- Controlled depth
- Do not ego lift
- Keep hips down on the pad

---

## 4. Seated or Lying Leg Curl

**3 sets x 10–15 reps**

Purpose:

- Hamstring hypertrophy
- Knee health
- Balanced leg development

Notes:

- Full squeeze
- Slow negative
- Do not use momentum

---

## 5. Standing Calf Raise

**4 sets x 8–15 reps**

Purpose:

- Calf growth
- Lower-leg balance
- Better proportions as a tall lifter

Notes:

- Deep stretch
- Full contraction
- Pause at top and bottom

---

## 6. Hanging Leg Raise or Cable Crunch

**3 sets x 10–15 reps**

Purpose:

- Abs
- Core strength
- Visible midsection once lean

Notes:

- Control the pelvis
- Do not swing
- For cable crunches, round the spine intentionally

---

# Day 3 — Upper Pull + Delts

## 1. Barbell Row or Chest-Supported Row

**3–4 sets x 6–10 reps**

Purpose:

- Back thickness
- Mid-back density
- Posture and frame

Notes:

- Chest-supported row is preferred if lower back fatigue is high
- Pull elbows back
- Do not jerk the weight

---

## 2. Seated Cable Row

**3 sets x 8–12 reps**

Purpose:

- Mid-back thickness
- Lat and upper-back development
- Posture

Notes:

- Pull through elbows
- Squeeze shoulder blades
- Do not lean excessively

---

## 3. Lat Pulldown or Pull-Up

**3 sets x 8–12 reps**

Purpose:

- Back width
- V-taper
- Lat development

Notes:

- Use a grip that feels natural
- Drive elbows downward
- Avoid turning it into a biceps movement

---

## 4. Rear Delt Fly or Reverse Pec Deck

**3 sets x 15–25 reps**

Purpose:

- Rear delt development
- More complete shoulder cap
- Upper-back detail and posture
- Shoulder balance

Good options:

- Reverse pec deck
- Cable rear delt fly
- Chest-supported dumbbell rear delt raise
- Rear delt row with elbows flared

Notes:

- Keep traps relaxed
- Move with control
- Prioritize the rear delts over load

---

## 5. Lateral Raise Variation

**3 sets x 12–25 reps**

Purpose:

- Shoulder width
- Capped delts
- High visual payoff

Good options:

- Dumbbell lateral raise
- Cable lateral raise
- Machine lateral raise
- Lean-away cable lateral raise

Notes:

- Clean reps
- Controlled movement
- High reps are fine
- Chase tension, not load

---

## 6. Hammer Curl or Incline Dumbbell Curl

**3 sets x 8–15 reps**

Purpose:

- Biceps
- Brachialis
- Forearms
- Arm thickness

Notes:

- Full range of motion
- Control the lowering
- Do not swing

---

# Day 4 — Zone 2 Cardio or Rest

Default choice:

- **25–40 minutes Zone 2 cardio**

Use complete rest instead if legs, joints, or sleep quality are clearly poor.

Good cardio options:

- Incline treadmill walk
- Bike
- Easy jog
- Rowing machine
- Stair machine, moderate effort

Zone 2 means you should be able to hold a conversation.

Optional add-on:

- 10–20 minutes easy incline walking after one upper-body day if you want a third low-fatigue cardio exposure

Do not turn this day into a hard conditioning session if Friday lower body performance suffers.

---

# Day 5 — Lower Hypertrophy + Athleticism

## 1. Trap-Bar Deadlift, Deadlift, or Hip Thrust

**2–3 sets x 3–6 reps**

Purpose:

- Posterior chain
- Strength
- Athletic density

Notes:

- Trap-bar deadlift is often a good option for tall lifters
- Keep volume low
- Heavy pulls are useful but fatiguing
- Do not grind every week

---

## 2. Bulgarian Split Squat

**3 sets x 8–12 reps per leg**

Purpose:

- Quads
- Glutes
- Balance
- Leg size without massive spinal loading

Notes:

- Controlled depth
- Slight forward lean is fine
- Keep front foot stable

---

## 3. Hack Squat, Leg Press, or Goblet Squat

**3 sets x 10–15 reps**

Purpose:

- Quad hypertrophy
- Leg volume
- Lower-body fullness

Notes:

- Choose the option that feels best on knees and hips
- Control reps
- Use a full range of motion

---

## 4. Leg Curl

**3 sets x 10–15 reps**

Purpose:

- Hamstrings
- Knee flexion strength
- Balanced lower body

Notes:

- Full squeeze
- Slow negative
- Keep hips stable

---

## 5. Calf Raise

**4 sets x 10–20 reps**

Purpose:

- Calf size
- Lower-leg balance

Notes:

- Pause in the stretched position
- Do not bounce
- Train calves seriously

---

## 6. Farmer Carries

**3–5 rounds x 30–60 seconds**

Purpose:

- Traps
- Grip
- Core
- Posture
- Overall “unit” look

Notes:

- Stand tall
- Walk controlled
- Use heavy dumbbells or a trap bar
- Do not let shoulders slump

---

## 7. Pallof Press, Ab Wheel, or Side Plank

**2–3 sets or rounds**

Purpose:

- Core strength
- Anti-extension or anti-rotation control
- Better athletic transfer than only crunching
- More complete waist and trunk development

Good options:

- Pallof press: 2–3 sets x 10–15 reps per side
- Ab wheel: 2–3 sets x 6–12 reps
- Side plank: 2–3 rounds x 30–60 seconds per side

Notes:

- Choose one option per session
- Keep reps controlled
- Do not chase fatigue at the expense of bracing quality
- This should not interfere with the next week of squats or deadlifts

---

# Day 6 — Upper Chest, Shoulders, and Arms

## 1. Incline Barbell or Dumbbell Press

**3–4 sets x 6–10 reps**

Purpose:

- Upper chest priority
- Main hypertrophy press
- Better torso aesthetics

Notes:

- Keep incline moderate
- Control the eccentric
- Progress gradually

---

## 2. Dumbbell Flat Press or Machine Chest Press

**2–3 sets x 8–12 reps**

Purpose:

- Chest thickness
- Pressing volume
- Shoulder-friendly hypertrophy

Notes:

- Use full range
- Controlled stretch
- Press evenly

---

## 3. Cable Fly, Low-to-High Cable Fly, or Pec Deck

**2–3 sets x 12–20 reps**

Purpose:

- Chest isolation
- Loaded stretch
- Chest shape and definition
- Extra upper-chest emphasis if using low-to-high cables

Notes:

- Keep tension on pecs
- Do not overbend the elbows
- Think about bringing upper arms together, not just hands

---

## 4. Lateral Raise

**3–4 sets x 15–25 reps**

Purpose:

- Side delt growth
- Shoulder width
- Capped-shoulder look

Notes:

- Use dumbbells, cables, or machine
- Keep reps clean
- Do not use body English
- Chase tension and control

---

## 5. Rear Delt Fly or Reverse Pec Deck

**2–3 sets x 15–25 reps**

Purpose:

- Rear delt growth
- Rounder shoulders from the side and back
- Shoulder balance and pressing health

Notes:

- Keep traps relaxed
- Pause briefly in the contracted position
- Use higher reps and clean tension

---

## 6. Overhead Triceps Extension

**3 sets x 10–20 reps**

Purpose:

- Long head of triceps
- Bigger arms from the side
- Complements pressing

Notes:

- Use cable, dumbbell, or EZ bar
- Get a deep stretch
- Keep elbows from flaring too much

---

## 7. Cable Curl or Hammer Curl

**3 sets x 10–15 reps**

Purpose:

- Biceps
- Brachialis
- Forearms
- Arm fullness

Notes:

- Control the lowering
- Avoid swinging
- Use a full range of motion

# Optional Overhead Press Guidance

Overhead pressing is useful, but it is not mandatory every week for this goal.

You already get significant front delt and triceps work from:

- Bench press
- Incline press
- Dumbbell or machine press
- Triceps isolation

For the target look, side delts and upper chest are more important than maximizing overhead press strength.

Use one of these options:

## Option 1 — Rotate Overhead Press Every Other Week

On Day 1, after incline dumbbell press, add:

**Overhead Press — 2 sets x 5–8 reps**

Then reduce triceps pushdowns or optional curls if time is tight.

## Option 2 — Use Machine Shoulder Press

On Day 6, after lateral raises or before them, add:

**Machine Shoulder Press — 2 sets x 8–12 reps**

Use this only if shoulders feel good.

## Option 3 — Skip Direct Overhead Press

This is acceptable if:

- Shoulders feel better without it
- Pressing volume is already progressing
- Side delts are growing
- Chest strength is improving

# Rep Ranges

## Big Compound Lifts

Use mostly:

- **3–6 reps** for strength
- **6–10 reps** for strength + size
- **8–12 reps** for hypertrophy

Examples:

- Bench press
- Squat
- Deadlift
- Rows
- Pull-ups
- Overhead press
- Incline press

## Isolation Lifts

Use mostly:

- **10–20 reps**
- **15–30 reps** for lateral raises, rear delts, calves, and some arm work

Examples:

- Curls
- Triceps pushdowns
- Triceps extensions
- Lateral raises
- Rear delt flyes
- Cable flyes
- Calf raises

## Practical Rule

For this goal, most work should be in the:

**6–15 rep range**

Then use higher reps for:

- Side delts
- Rear delts
- Arms
- Calves
- Flyes

---

# Training Intensity

Most working sets should end with:

**1–3 reps in reserve**

That means you could do 1–3 more clean reps if forced.

## Heavy Compounds

Usually stop with:

**1–3 reps in reserve**

Do not max out often.

Do not grind heavy reps every week.

## Isolation Exercises

You can go closer to failure:

**0–2 reps in reserve**

Good exercises to push hard:

- Lateral raises
- Cable curls
- Triceps pushdowns
- Overhead triceps extensions
- Pec deck
- Cable flyes
- Calf raises

---

# Progression System

Use **double progression**.

Example:

Incline dumbbell press  
**3 sets x 8–12 reps**

Start with a weight you can do for:

- Set 1: 10 reps
- Set 2: 9 reps
- Set 3: 8 reps

Keep the same weight until you can hit something like:

- Set 1: 12 reps
- Set 2: 12 reps
- Set 3: 11–12 reps

Then increase the weight next time.

## Load Increases

Use conservative jumps:

- Upper-body barbell lifts: +5 lb
- Lower-body barbell lifts: +5–10 lb
- Dumbbells: smallest available jump
- Cables/machines: one plate when you own the top of the rep range

Do not add weight if form breaks down.

Examples:

# Cardio

To stay lean and athletic, do:

**2–3 cardio sessions per week**

Default weekly setup:

- Thursday: 25–40 minutes Zone 2
- Optional: 10–20 minutes easy incline walking after one upper-body day
- Optional: short intervals once weekly if recovery is good

Good options:

- Incline walking: 25–45 minutes
- Bike: 20–40 minutes
- Rowing: 10–25 minutes
- Sled pushes: short intervals
- Sports: basketball, boxing, soccer, etc.

Most cardio should be easy to moderate.

One harder conditioning session per week is fine if recovery is good.

Hard conditioning should not reduce lower-body performance or make Monday’s upper-body session worse.

---

# Optional Conditioning Session

Use only if recovery is good.

Example:

- 5–10 minute warm-up
- 8 rounds:
  - 20 seconds hard
  - 100 seconds easy
- 5 minute cooldown

Good tools:

- Bike
- Rowing machine
- Sled push
- Incline treadmill
- Battle ropes
- Hill sprints, if joints tolerate them

Do not add this if:

- Leg performance is dropping
- Sleep quality is poor
- Joints feel beat up
- Appetite is poor
- Strength is stalling across multiple lifts

---

## Calories

Your calorie target depends on your current body fat.

### If Already Fairly Lean

Use a small surplus:

**+200 to +300 calories/day**

Goal:

- Gain muscle
- Keep waist controlled
- Avoid dirty bulk

### If Carrying Extra Body Fat

Use maintenance or a slight deficit:

**-200 to -300 calories/day**

Goal:

- Recomp
- Lose fat slowly
- Still gain strength and muscle
