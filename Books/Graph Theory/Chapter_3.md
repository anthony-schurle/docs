## 3.1 2-Connected Graphs And Subgraphs

---

### Definitions

- **Block**: Maximal connected subgraph without a cutvertex. So either a maximal 2-connected subgraph, bridge with its ends, or an isolated vertex.
- **Block Graph**: If $A$ is the set of cutvertices and $\mathcal{B}$ the set of blocks for graph $G$, the block graph of $G$ is made of vertex set $A \cup \mathcal{B}$ with edges $aB$ when $a \in B$.

### Results & Proofs

**Proposition: Proposition 3.1.1.**
A graph is 2-connected iff it can be constructed from a cycle by successively adding $H$-paths to graphs $H$ already constructed.

**Lemma: Lemma 3.1.2**
Let $G$ be any graph.
(i) The cycles in $G$ are precisely the cycles in its blocks.
(ii) The bonds of $G$ are precisely the bonds of its blocks.

**Lemma: Lemma 3.1.3**
The following statements are equivalent for distinct edges $e, f$ of a graph $G$:
(i) The edges $e, f$ belong to a common block of $G$.
(ii) The edges $e, f$ belong to a common cycle in $G$.
(iii) The edges $e, f$ belong to a common bond of $G$.

**Lemma: Lemma 3.1.4**
The block graph of a connected graph is a tree.

## 3.2 The Structure Of 3-Connected Graphs

---

### Definitions

- **Wheel**: $C^n * K^1$.

### Results & Proofs

**Lemma: Lemma 3.2.1**
Let $e$ be an edge in graph $G$. If $G \dot{-} e$ (the graph obtained from suppressing any end of $e$ that has degree 2 in $G-e$) is 3-connected, then so is $G$.

**Lemma: Lemma 3.2.2**
Every 3-connected graph $G \ne K^4$ has an edge $e$ such that $G \dot{-} e$ is another 3-connected graph.
> Intuition: $G$ must contain a $TK^4$. Pick any maximal edge $TJ$ where $J \ne G$ is 3-connected. There must exist a TJ-path between vertices not in the same subdivision, else we can create a larger $TJ$. Consider $TJ \cup P$ with this path, suppressing vertices. 

**Theorem: Theorem 3.2.3**
A graph $G$ is 3-connected iff there exists a sequence $G_0, ..., G_n$ of graphs such that
(i) $G_0 = K^4$ and $G_n = G$.
(ii) $G_{i+1}$ has an edge $e$ such that $G_i = G_{i+1} \dot{-} e$ for every $i < n$.
Moreover, the graphs in any such sequence are all 3-connected.

**Lemma: Lemma 3.2.4**
Every 3-connected graph $G \ne K^4$ has an edge $e$ such that $G / e$ is again 3-connected.
> Intuition: If not, every edge is part of a 3 vertex separation. This causes infinite regression into smaller components. 

**Theorem: Theorem 3.2.5**
A graph is 3-connected iff there exists a sequence $G_0, ..., G_n$ of graphs with the following two properties:
(i) $G_0 = K^4$ and $G_n = G$;
(ii) $G_{i+1}$ has an edge $xy$ such that $d(x), d(y) \ge 3$ and $G_i = G_{i+1}/xy$, for every $i < n$.
Moreover, the graphs in such a sequence are all 3-connected.

**Theorem: Theorem 3.2.6**
The cycle space of every 3-connected graph $G$ is generated by its non-separating induced cycles.
