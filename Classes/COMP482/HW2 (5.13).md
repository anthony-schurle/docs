**Problem (11b, 11d, 11f)**:
For each of the following languages $L$:
(i) Describe the equivalence classes of $\approx_L$.
(ii) If the number of equivalence classes of $\approx_L$ is finite, construct the minimal DFSM that accepts L.
b) 
$$
\left\{
w \in \{0,1\}^*
\;\middle|\;
\begin{array}{l}
w \text{ has either an odd number of 1's and an odd number of 0's,} \\
\text{or it has an even number of 1's and an even number of 0's}
\end{array}
\right\}
$$

d) $\{ww^R | w \in \{a, b\}^*\}$.
f) $\{w \in \{0, 1\}^* | \text{there is no occurrence of the substring 000 in w}\}$.
**Solution**:
b)
i)
$$
\begin{align}
&[1] : [\text{all strings w such that |w| is even}] \\
&[2] : [\text{all strings w such that |w| is odd}]
\end{align}
$$
ii)
The minimal DFSM defined by $\approx_L$ is $M = (\{[1], [2]\}, \{0, 1\}, \{(([1], 0), [2]), (([1], 1), [2]), (([2], 0), [1]), (([2], 1), [1])\}, [1], \{[1]\})$.
(Here we relabel the states $[i] = q_{i-1}$ for all $i \in \mathbb{N}$ where $1 \le i \le 2$)
![[HW5.13.11b.jpg]]

d) 
i)
Notice $\forall i, j \in \mathbb{N}$ where $i \ne j$, we have $[a^ib] \ne [a^jb]$. This follows by setting $w = ba^i$ so that $(a^ib)w = a^ibba^i \in L$ but $(a^jb)w = a^jbba^i \not \in L$. Hence, there are infinite equivalence classes of $\approx_L$.
ii)
$L$ is not a regular language since the equivalence classes of $\approx_L$ are infinite.

f)
i)
$$
\begin{align}
&[1] : [\text{all strings (possibly empty) that do not end in 0 and do not contain 000}] \\
&[2] : [\text{all strings ending in exactly one 0 and do not contain 000}] \\
&[3] : [\text{all strings ending in two consecutive 0's and do not contain 000}] \\
&[4] : [\text{all strings containing the substring 000}]
\end{align}
